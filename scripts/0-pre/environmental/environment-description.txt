X_ENVIRONMENT.CSV: PROCESSING AND VARIABLE DICTIONARY

CONTENTS
One row per atlas grid cell and atlas period: 2,165 cells x 3 atlases =
6,495 rows. There are 21 columns. All supplied grid cells are retained;
shared bird-survey coverage filters must be applied downstream.
Values are not standardised. NA means missing, not zero.

CODE AND PROCESSING
Entry point (run from the repository root):
  scripts/0-pre/preparing-environment.R
Helpers:
  scripts/0-pre/environmental/process-hilda.R
  scripts/0-pre/environmental/process-chelsacruts.R

The code reads the original Danish 5-km atlas grid and annual HILDA+ v2
land-use/cover rasters. It crops the rasters to the grid extent and calculates
class areas using exact polygon overlap fractions multiplied by pixel area.
Original classes remain separate: forest and crop types are not combined.

CHELSAcruts V1.0 monthly tmin, tmax and precipitation rasters are read directly
from the remote source and cropped to the grid extent, with local crops cached
for reuse. The grid polygons are transformed to the raster CRS; the raster
pixels are not resampled. Climate extraction uses exact overlaps weighted by
pixel area. Source temperature values are divided by 10 to obtain degrees C;
precipitation is unscaled. Source -32768 pixels are treated as missing.
No previously prepared climate shapefiles are used.

Breeding season is March-June. Monthly mean temperature is (tmin+tmax)/2.
The four monthly temperatures are averaged with equal weights; the four
monthly precipitation totals are summed. These annual breeding-season metrics
are then averaged over the climate years for each atlas. Missing months and
years are not silently omitted. Spatial means exclude missing raster pixels.

HILDA percentages and heterogeneity are calculated separately for each year
and then averaged over the atlas years. Heterogeneity is the mean of annual
diversity values, not diversity calculated from period-averaged percentages.
Climate and land-cover summaries are joined using the grid-cell and atlas IDs.
Area diagnostics and temporal metadata remain in the intermediate per-atlas
folders rather than in the final predictor matrix.

PERIODS
Atlas 1: HILDA 1971-1974; climate 1971-1974.
Atlas 2: HILDA 1993-1996; climate 1993-1996.
Atlas 3: HILDA 2014-2017; climate 2014-2016.
CHELSAcruts ends in 2016; no 2017 climate values are imputed.

VARIABLES
survey          Unique site-atlas key, formatted <kvadratkod>_<atlas>.
kvadratkod      Original atlas grid-cell identifier.
atlas           Atlas number: 1, 2 or 3.
tmean_breeding  Mean March-June temperature, degrees C.
prec_breeding   Total March-June precipitation, mm per breeding season,
                averaged across the climate years.
hh              Simpson compositional diversity: 1 - sum(p_i^2), where p_i
                is the area proportion of original HILDA class i. ALL 15
                classes contribute, including ocean and inland water.
                No source classes are merged. Higher values indicate more
                compositional diversity; this is not a fragmentation index.
                With 15 equally represented classes the maximum is 14/15;
                a single-class cell has zero diversity.
pct_class_*     Percentage coverage (0-100) of each original HILDA class.
                The denominator is all valid sampled cell area, including
                ocean and inland water. Missing source pixels (99 or NA)
                are excluded. Complete rows sum to 100% across 15 classes.

IMPORTANT: pct_class_44 is deciduous broadleaf forest only, not total forest.
Likewise pct_class_22 is annual crops only, not all agricultural classes.
Any subsequent combination of classes is left to the analyst.

CHECKS AND LIMITATIONS
X_environment_missingness.csv reports missing values by column.
The current matrix contains 21 missing temperature records and nine missing
precipitation records. FD02 lacks temperature for all three atlases despite
having more than 25% HILDA land area. Missing values have not been imputed.
Precipitation differs materially from the legacy environmental matrix; the
cause of those legacy differences has not been established. Earlier expanded
figures and comparison tables may include variables removed from this final
matrix. Existing downstream model inputs have not been overwritten.
